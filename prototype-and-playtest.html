prototype & playtest

A collection of games produced over a semester.

In January – May 2026, I and three others participated in a workshop course in which we built, playtested, and presented new games every week.

We used PICO-8, a game engine with intentional limitations (a 16-color palette, a 128×128 sprite sheet, a code token limit) intended to encourage rapid prototyping and breed creativity. We were inspired by Adam Saltsman's approach to making small games.

Some of the games I created are embedded below!

samsara

Pay off your karmic debt through the cycle of rebirth...

I worried about microgame parsability, so every microgame has text prompts a la WarioWare that help get you on your feet. If I were to change something, I'd move the prompts out of the periphery nearer the things they affect, since some playtesters took some time to grasp the input schemes.

sakura high

Make time for love -- and work?! -- at Sakura High...

A visual novel where you have to fit events into free time. I made the calendar screen first and played with it until it felt right. Based on my experience last week, I placed input hints near their area of effect and no playtester had a problem with the input scheme. I felt so smart for that.

campus dining

Prove yourself as the king of campus dining! If you dare...

This is inspired by my university's lofty position on the US campus food ranking, which they like to placard everywhere (a little comically).

go fishin'

A fast-paced arcade-style fishing game.

For our last session, the four of us made a single game together: a fishing game in which you collect fish versions of all the games we made, and can launch them... with many additional features (including a secret boss battle). Our professor was overjoyed.

This is a standalone version that just includes the fishing game. Some fish sprites are by my fellow game-makers. Music and sound effects are by the fantastic Kate Lyman.
